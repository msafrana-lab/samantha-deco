Projects & visualisations

Six interiors redesigned — explore with the slider

Every project in this gallery is a Rives Intérieures design: first the room as it is so often found in this region, then its transformation — exactly the visualisation work I do for your home before any purchase. That is my method: see before you do.

Before After

Living room · 32 m²French shore spirit

The living room that turned its back on the lake

Heavy curtains, a massive sofa, an ignored view: everything faced the television. We reverse the polarity — freed glazing, mineral tones, discreet brass — and the whole room starts celebrating Lake Geneva.

Before After

Kitchen · 14 m²Village house

The Chablais kitchen brought out of the shadows

The orange oak of the 1980s absorbed all the light. Fronts repainted in sage, a pale wood worktop, artisan zellige and open shelving: the same kitchen, without replacing everything — and the village visible again through the window.

Before After

Bedroom · 16 m²Cross-border life

Sleeping and working from home, finally separated

The folding table at the foot of the bed — the emblem of remote work endured. We design an ink-and-brass desk niche, a real cane headboard, suspended bedside lights: in the evening the screen switches off and the bedroom becomes a bedroom again.

Before After

Studio · 24 m²Home staging

The studio made sale-ready in one day

A buyer does not visit square metres — they visit a promise. Radical decluttering, legible zones (sleep, eat, host), a mirror doubling the light: the listing changes category, using only the furniture already there.

Before After

Entryway · 6 m²Small spaces

Six square metres that set the tone

The entryway is the home's trailer. Full-height storage that swallows coats and shoes, a floating bench, brass hooks, graphic tiles: the first impression stops being an apology ("don't mind the mess!") and becomes a pride.

Before After

Living room · 38 m²Alpine spirit

The chalet that kept its soul, not its gloom

Timber on every surface and a low-mood ceiling: the classic chalet trap. We paint the walls cream, keep the wood overhead, swap the massive fireplace for a graphite stove — the alpine spirit stays, the suffocation goes.
