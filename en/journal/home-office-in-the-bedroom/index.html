Home office

Working from home: a real desk in the bedroom without sacrificing sleep

It is THE request that has soared in this region: cross-border workers now work from home part of the week, and the folding table wedged between bed and wardrobe has had its day.

The real problem is not space — it is the boundary

A desk in the bedroom raises a question of territory: how do you stop work from colonising sleep? Research converges on this — a workstation visible from the bed degrades sleep onset. The answer is not to give up; it is to mark the boundary.

Three set-ups that work

The assumed niche

Rather than blending the desk into the décor, give it a stage: one wall section painted in a deep tone (ink blue, forest green), a full-width worktop, two shelves. In the evening, a curtain or screen closes the parenthesis. The colour contrast tells the brain: this is another place.

The closet office

A well-designed “cloffice” closes completely: doors shut, work literally disappears. Ideal in bedrooms under 14 m², provided the inside is properly wired (fixed power strip, cable grommets) so that closing it is not a nightly chore.

The thickened headboard

In elongated bedrooms, a 35 cm-deep partition headboard hides a worktop on its back. You sleep on one side, work on the other — and from the pillow, the screen does not exist.

The infallible test: lying in bed, if you can see the screen, the boundary does not yet exist.

The details that change everything

  • The chair: a genuine task chair is non-negotiable, but it can be beautiful — the market is full of ergonomic armchairs with nothing “office” about them.
  • The light: an adjustable 4000 K task lamp for work, warm 2700 K bulbs everywhere else. Two lighting worlds, one room.
  • The video-call backdrop: plan it from the start — one well-styled wall behind the chair beats any blurred background.

This project is one of my classics as a Full Project: it is exactly the case where visualising beforehand avoids the costly mistake — you see at once whether the niche crushes the room or organises it.
