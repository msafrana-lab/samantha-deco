Living by Lake Geneva: how to (finally) reveal the view

Every week I walk into lake-view apartments that live with their curtains drawn. It is the great regional paradox: people pay a premium for the view, then arrange the living room as if it did not exist.

1. Point the seating at the light, not the screen

In most living rooms I visit, the sofa faces the television and turns its back on the window. The first and most profitable decision: rotate the plan. Sofa perpendicular to the glazing, one armchair looking at the lake, the screen on a secondary wall. You are not giving up television — you are simply no longer giving it the best seat in the house.

2. Undress the window

Heavy double curtains, inherited from the days of poor glazing, eat 20 to 30 cm of view on each side. Replace them with a sheer that filters without blocking, hung on a rail that extends well beyond the frame: once open, the curtains free the entire pane.

The rule I give my clients: from the sofa, you should see water — not fabric.

3. Work with mineral tones

Lake Geneva dictates its own palette: blue-greys in the morning, steel at noon, almost golden at sunset. The walls framing it should accompany it, not compete. Warm cream, greige, the softest sage — and bright colours in small, movable touches such as cushions and ceramics.

4. Beware of tall furniture near the glazing

A bookcase or tall sideboard within a metre of the window creates a visual tunnel. The zone along the glazing should stay low: a low sideboard, a bench, or nothing at all. A view needs a landing strip.

5. The mirror: your second window

Placed on the wall perpendicular to the glazing, a large mirror catches the lake and throws it back into the room. It works wonders in deep living rooms whose far end never sees the water. The cheapest trick on this list — and often the most spectacular.

Putting it into practice

These five principles are the backbone of most of my lake-view projects. The difficulty is not knowing them — it is applying them to your volumes: where to rotate the sofa when a radiator blocks the ideal wall, how to dress a four-metre window without a monumental budget.
