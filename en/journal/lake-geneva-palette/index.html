Colours

Sage, brass, deep blue: the palette that suits Lake Geneva so well

Every region has its light, and every light has its allied colours. Lake Geneva's — soft, changing, reflected by the water — has its own. It is no accident that three tones keep returning in my projects.

Understanding the light here

Lake Geneva light is reflected light: the water adds a cool, shifting component; the mountains cut it early in the evening. As a result, pure whites turn sad-grey from 5 pm, and highly saturated tones look garish at noon. The palette that works here must absorb these variations with grace.

Sage green: the neutral that is not grey

Sage behaves like a neutral — it goes with everything — but it is alive: greenish in sunlight, almost grey-blue at dusk. It is the perfect tone for the region's kitchens and bedrooms, on lower walls, cabinet fronts or bed linen. It echoes the shores without ever imitating them.

Brass: solid light

As the days shorten, brass — hooks, frames, taps, lamp bases — does what chrome cannot: it warms. Strict dosage: it is a condiment, not a dish. Three to five brass points per room are enough; beyond that, you tip into hotel lobby.

Brass in a room is bottled sunset.

Deep blue: the lake's echo

Ink, petrol or midnight — deep blue gives what beige never will: gravity. A niche wall, a headboard, a dressing room, an entire cloakroom: it excels in defined uses, in dialogue with pale wood and cream. Facing a lake-side window, it creates an indoor-outdoor continuity that is uncannily right.

The dosing formula

  • 70% warm light background (cream, greige, off-white);
  • 20% anchoring tones (sage and/or deep blue, wood);
  • 10% accents (brass, ceramics, textiles).

This 70/20/10 rule is nothing original — its local application is what changes everything: here, the 70% must be warm (never pure white), and the 20% benefits from echoing the landscape. It is exactly the kind of calibration we settle together during an Advice Visit, samples in hand, in YOUR light.
